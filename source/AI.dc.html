How the River uses AI

AI, held with care

The Relational River is about relationship with the living world, and no machine can do that for us. Here, in plain words, is where AI is used in the River, where it is not, what it costs, and the care we take.

Last updated 23 November 2026. These commitments are living, and we will revisit them each year with partners and people who use the River.

What you meet in the River

No AI runs in what you see and use here.

  • Raven, your guide, speaks words written by people, not generated by a machine.
  • The waterfall matches your words with offerings already in the River, using simple code that runs in your own browser. What you write there is not saved or sent anywhere.
  • The Well keeps what you choose to share privately, for people to read, never for AI to learn from.

Where AI helps behind the scenes

AI helps tend the River quietly, in a few places:

  • Building the River. The website is built with help from Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. People at the Institute of Relational Being decide what the River is and how it speaks.
  • Springs and threads. Once a week, an AI run reads public pages to find new offerings with a closing date, checks each one against the River’s ethos guide, and adds those that fit. Everything it adds is labelled Noticed by the River and links to the organisers’ own page.
  • A weekly note. The same run drafts the Institute of Relational Being’s weekly newsletter, which a person reads and edits before it is shared.

Our commitments

  1. AI only where it earns its place. Before using AI for anything, we ask whether a simpler way would serve as well. Much of the River works without it.
  2. Small and close to home. If AI ever appears in what you use, we will prefer small tools that run on your own device over large ones in the cloud.
  3. Treading lightly. We keep AI use to what is needed, such as one gathering run a week, and prefer using less over offsetting.
  4. People decide. Anything AI writes about an organisation is labelled, linked to its source, and open to correction or removal. Our aim is for a person to review every new spring.
  5. Permission first. We use only what organisations share publicly, invite them to shape their own entries, and never publish the location of a sensitive species or a sacred place.
  6. Some knowledge stays out of reach. We never use AI to gather, summarise or generate Indigenous ceremony, language, stories or traditional ecological knowledge. Indigenous voices are honoured and illuminated wherever possible, in their own words and linked to their own sites.
  7. Never training on people. Your pictures, words and offerings in the River are never used to train AI models, ours or anyone else’s.
  8. Out in the open. This page says what we do. If anything changes, it changes here first.

What belongs in the Springs

Every spring, whether shared by a person or noticed by the River, should be in resonance with the Institute of Relational Being’s ethos. A spring belongs when it:

  • deepens relationship with the living world, meeting rivers, trees, creatures, places and Moon as Beings, not resources;
  • supports reciprocity and care, between people and with the rest of nature;
  • is grounded in place, community or an understanding of living systems;
  • honours Indigenous peoples, sharing Indigenous-led offerings in their own words, and teachings of ceremony or traditional knowledge only when their keepers lead or endorse them;
  • is honest and clear, with a real organiser, a clear cost and a working link.

We leave out greenwashing, high-pressure selling, appropriation of Indigenous knowledge, and anything that cannot be checked. Free, by-donation and paid offerings are all welcome, and we especially value those that make room for people with fewer resources.

The cost we carry

AI is not weightless. Much of its energy and water use comes from large data centres, and building it draws on the Earth’s materials. The River’s own footprint is small, but it is real, and we would rather name it than hide it. It is one reason we keep AI behind the scenes, and use it sparingly.

Learning with others

These commitments were shaped in conversation with the questions raised by the BioFi Project in Can AI Catalyze the Regeneration of Place?, with gratitude. They ask when AI is worth its costs, who decides what local knowledge becomes data, and how communities keep control of it. We hold those questions as our own.
