How the River cares for your information

The River asks for very little. You can walk all of it without giving your name, and nothing about your visit is tracked. This notice explains, in plain words, what is kept when you choose to share something, why, for how long, and the rights you have. It is written to meet the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and we offer the same care to everyone, wherever you live.

Last updated 24 November 2026, as IRB accounts open. This notice is being reviewed before the River fully opens.

Your Boat

Your Boat is your own vessel on the River: a private place for where you began, the practices you tend, your constellation and what you gather along the way. It is entirely optional, and there is nothing in the River you need a Boat to use.

Kept on your device. When you build a Boat on this device, everything in it stays in your browser on that device. Nothing is sent to the Institute of Relational Being, and nobody else can see it. You can download a copy at any time, restore from a copy, or sink your Boat, which deletes everything in it from your device straight away.

Your Boat’s wake. Once you have a Boat, it remembers which waters of the River you visit, one mark per water each day, so that “Launch your Boat” can sail them with you and reflect on your journey. The wake lives inside your Boat, wherever your Boat is kept, and goes nowhere else. It records only which waters you visited and on which day. Alongside it, your Boat keeps a logbook of the films, talks, readings and offerings you open from the River: just each one’s title, link, kind and the water it came from, so you can find it again and your Boat can remember it with you. Nothing about what you do on other websites is ever seen. You can pause both, let go of single items, or clear them at any time from the Boat page.

The Harbour. If you keep your Boat with an IRB account, you can choose to moor it in the Harbour, a public page for collaborators open to connecting. Only what you write on your Harbour card is shown there: a name, what you are working on, how others could join in, and, if you add them, a rough place, a few themes and a link to your Hylo profile. Nothing else from your Boat is shared. Anyone can raise a concern about a card; concerns are seen only by the Institute of Relational Being’s stewards, and a card with several concerns in a week rests out of view until a steward looks at it. You can update your card or leave the Harbour at any time, and leaving removes it straight away. Sinking your Boat or deleting your account removes your card too.

Lunar journal reminders. If you ask for a nightly reminder for your lunar journal, the River makes a small calendar file on your device for you to add to your own calendar. It is not sent to us, we cannot see it, and you stop it by deleting the event in your calendar.

Kept with your IRB account. If you choose, your Boat can be kept with an Institute of Relational Being account, so it sails with you from device to device. You sign in with a one-time code sent to your email, with no password. We hold your email address and what you put in your Boat, in the Institute’s account home run by Supabase on servers in Ireland, in the European Union. The database itself lets each collaborator reach only their own Boat. Nobody at the Institute looks inside Boats: the only people who could, through Supabase’s tools, are the Institute’s account stewards, and only if you ask us for help or the law requires it. Your Boat is never sold, shared or used to train AI.

A copy of your Boat also stays on the device you use, so it opens quickly, until you sign out. You can download everything at any time. Sinking your Boat deletes it from your account and that device, and your account stays open. Deleting your IRB account removes your account and everything kept with it, including anything in ReBe, straight away; any copies in routine backups expire automatically. The same account can open ReBe, the Institute’s app, if you would like it to, and you never need one to use the other.

AI and your information

Your information in the River is never used to train AI models, and nothing you write in the waterfall or pour into the Well is sent to an AI. To learn where AI does help tend the River, and the care we take, see How the River uses AI.

Who it is shared with

We never sell your information, never use it for advertising, and never share it for anyone else’s marketing.

  • Cloudflare, Inc. hosts the website and the Well’s database, as our processor under its Data Processing Addendum, and may only use the information to provide that service.
  • Supabase runs the Institute’s account home, on servers in Ireland, as our processor under its Data Processing Addendum. It holds IRB accounts and Boats kept with them.
  • Resend sends the one-time sign-in codes, as our processor under its Data Processing Addendum. It receives only your email address and the code.
  • Projects and constellations, only when you say yes to an invitation, and only what you agree to share.
  • River stewards, a few trusted people who help tend the Well, under a duty of confidentiality.
  • Authorities, only if the law truly requires it.

Information travelling beyond Europe

IRB and Cloudflare are based in the United States, so information may be handled there. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK extension, and its Data Processing Addendum also includes the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, so your information keeps the protection GDPR requires.

IRB accounts and Boats are stored in the European Union. Supabase’s support and operations teams may reach them from the United States or Singapore, and Resend, which sends the sign-in codes, is based in the United States. Both protect these transfers with the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, as part of their Data Processing Addendums.

Cookies and your browser

The River uses no cookies for tracking, analytics or advertising, and stores nothing in your browser unless you ask it to, so there is no cookie banner to click through. If you build a Boat on this device, its contents are kept in your browser’s local storage, only there, and only until you sink it. Cloudflare may set one strictly necessary security cookie, __cf_bm, which helps tell people from automated bots, is not used to track you, and expires after 30 minutes of inactivity. If this ever changes, we will ask you first.

Your rights

Under GDPR you have the right to:

  • see what we hold about you, and receive a copy;
  • correct anything that is wrong;
  • delete your information;
  • limit how we use it, or object to uses based on our legitimate interests;
  • take it with you, in a common file format;
  • withdraw your consent at any time, without affecting what happened before.

Your offering in the Well comes with a private link where you can read it, download a copy and withdraw it yourself, straight away. For anything else, write to privacy@relationalriver.org. It is free, and we will answer within one month. We may ask a question to make sure a request really comes from you.

The River may suggest people for a project, but it never makes decisions about you on its own: nothing happens without your yes.

If you have a concern

Please tell us first, and we will do our best to put it right. You can also complain to a data protection authority, in the country where you live or work, or where you think something went wrong. In Ireland, that is the Data Protection Commission; in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office. The European Data Protection Board lists the authority for every EU country.

Young people

Anyone may visit the River. The Well is for people aged 16 and over, and we do not knowingly keep information about anyone younger. If you think a young person has poured in an offering, please write to us and we will delete it.

Keeping it safe

The site is only served over a secure connection. Each private link is stored only in scrambled form, so even we cannot rebuild it, and only stewards who need to can read offerings. If something ever went wrong in a way that put your information at risk, we would tell the relevant authority within 72 hours, and tell you directly if the risk to you were high.

Changes

If this notice changes, we will update the date above. If a change affects what you agreed to, we will ask you again before it applies to you.

We try to tend your information the way we hope to tend the places we love: gently, honestly, and only taking what is needed.
